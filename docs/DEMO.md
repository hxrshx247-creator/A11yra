# A11yra demo

Use the generated [Study Group Syllabus](../backend/fixtures/syllabus.docx). It is a Word handout with an informative attendance chart and one deliberately missing description.

## Three-minute run-through

1. **Show the barrier.** Open the syllabus and find the chart under **Attendance example**. Its bars compare 12 students on Monday with 24 on Thursday. Ask how a student who cannot see the bars would learn those values.
2. **Find and fix it.** Upload the original syllabus. Audit progress is **5 of 6 checks passed**. Open **Image alt text**, select **Review fix**, and choose **Conveys information**. Enter “Attendance doubled from 12 students on Monday to 24 on Thursday.” Apply the fix. The finding now says **Fixed, awaiting verification**.
3. **Verify the change.** Return to the audit, choose **Re-audit document**, and run it. The comparison shows **6 of 6 checks passed** and **Verified fixed (1)**. Explain that this confirms the description is present. A teacher still judges whether it communicates the chart.
4. **Switch to the learner.** Continue to export. Select **Offline HTML (.html)** and choose **Download for students**. Open the saved file in Edge with networking disabled. Tab to **Contents**, open it if needed, and choose **Attendance example**. Show that focus lands on the heading. Tab to **Image description**, then press Enter to reveal the teacher’s wording.
5. **Show reading options.** Open **Reading settings** and try larger text and high contrast. Demonstrate read-along only if an installed local voice is available. Use the remaining time to explain the scope.

## Slide outline

| Slide | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Who struggles? | Students who navigate with a screen reader or keyboard still receive Word handouts. |
| What is the barrier? | The attendance chart carries two numbers that the surrounding text never supplies. |
| What changes? | A teacher approves a description, a new audit verifies it is present, and the learner opens the result offline. |
| What needs work? | Validate with learners and screen readers, improve coverage of complex Word layouts, and review the quality of descriptions and reading order. |
